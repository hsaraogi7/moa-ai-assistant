# Micro Offer: Rules, Pricing, Naming, Scorecard

## Definitions
- "An offer is a solution to a specific problem packaged in exchange for money." No price = just content. [Source: MOA S1 slides]
- Micro offer = $1-$97 digital product solving ONE problem for ONE person in the fastest way. It should feel like an impulse buy. [Source: MOA S1 slides, Tool 1 pack]
- Formats: PDF, cheat sheet, template, mini course, prompt pack, toolkit, framework, swipe file, blueprint. "Simpler format = faster consumption = faster results = better testimonials." [Source: MOA S1 slides]

## Bad vs good offer statements [Source: MOA S1 slides, Tool 1 pack]
- "My offer is coaching." -> "My offer helps coaches get 3 paid clients in 30 days using organic content."
- "My offer is a course on Instagram." -> "A $27 guide that shows service providers how to turn 1 Instagram post into 5 DM conversations."
- "I help people with their mindset." -> "I help burnt-out entrepreneurs identify and break the one belief keeping them stuck at the same income level."

## The 4 parts of an irresistible micro offer [Source: MOA S1 slides]
1. Specific outcome ("Get 3 paid buyers in 7 days", not "grow your business").
2. Clear time frame (7 days, 2 hours, 30 minutes. Faster is more compelling).
3. Why YOU (your method, your result, your story).
4. Simple delivery (PDF, cheat sheet, template).

## 5 things a daily buyer offer must have [Source: 1SAD masterclass]
1. Taps a problem people are ALREADY paying to solve.
2. Solves something URGENT, not something future ("build muscle in 30 days" beats "train for longevity").
3. Promises a believable result. "A small, fully solved problem converts better than a big half-solved one."
4. Gives a faster OR easier result.
5. Has a unique angle on a proven problem (same product = compete on price, fresh angle = win on value).

## Quick-win checks [Source: Micro-Offer Generator, Tool 1 pack]
- Buyer gets a first result in 15 minutes or less (the generator's core promise). First real result within 1-3 days.
- Consumable in under about 3 hours.
- Buildable fast: 1-2 hours with AI. Use free or cheap tools (Google Docs, Canva, Loom).
- Never tell a member they need a website, complicated tech, weeks of time, a big audience or designers.
- Lead with the outcome, not the mechanism ("full chairs", not "ads").

## Price bands
Front-end types [Source: MOA S1 slides]:
| Type | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Digital book / guide | $5-$17 | 20-50 pages on one thing |
| Mini workshop | $17-$47 | One session, one result |
| Templates / swipe files | $5-$47 | The exact thing to use |
| Toolkit / bundle | under $50 | Several assets, ONE topic |
| Assessment / audit | up to $97 | Personalization is the value. Good for service providers |

Micro-Offer Generator tiers [Source: Micro-Offer Generator]: $7 = single checklist or template. $17 = toolkit of 3-5 deliverables. $27 = complete framework, 5+ items plus training.
- Under about $50: no lead magnet needed, image ads are enough. Near $100 or above: a 10-12 page doc or a VSL helps build trust. [Source: Sahil Q&A]

## Pain vs desire [Source: MOA S1 slides, Sahil Q&A]
- Pain buyer: "I'm stuck, frustrated, wasting time." Reacts to deadlines, scarcity, urgency. Formula: "Stop X in Y time".
- Desire buyer: "I want to become, achieve, gain." Reacts to vision, status, exclusivity. Formula: "Become Z by Y time".
- Which one depends on WHO you serve and your goal, not the price. Big pool of beginners = pain. Six-figure earners = desire / scale. Hybrids exist ("making money but hate sales calls").
- Painkiller messaging = emotional, higher urgency, broader market. Vitamin = aspirational, serious buyers, smaller market. "Messaging determines lead quality more than targeting."
- Tangible beats intangible at the start. Tie intangible niches to a tangible outcome ("cut 40 hours a week to 20 with the same outcome").

## Awareness stages (Schwartz) [Source: MOA S1 slides]
1. Unaware: relatable observation, no pitch.
2. Problem aware: call out the pain ("not you, your system"). Biggest audience.
3. Solution aware: position your approach as different.
4. Product aware: urgency + scarcity + social proof.
5. Most aware: remove friction ("Comment MOA").

## Finding the offer
- 4 questions: what does the audience already want, what quick win can you deliver, what leads naturally to the backend, what can you create or repurpose fast. [Source: MOA S1 slides]
- First place to look: how did previous clients first find or pay you? That is usually the first micro offer. [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Micro-Offer Generator flow (ask ONE question at a time) [Source: Micro-Offer Generator]:
  1. Zone of Genius Diagnostic: skills inventory, transformation mapping, advice patterns, natural talents, market check. Output 3-5 topics.
  2. Demand Validation Matrix: avatar precision, 5-7 urgent pain points, market proof, need vs want, Go/No-Go.
  3. 15-Minute Transformation Framework: 3 offers at $7-$27, each with name, core promise, named unique mechanism ("The 3-Message Method"), specific deliverable stack ("10 copy-paste DM scripts"), no-brainer price, 3-step process, quick-win factor.
  4. Offer Refinement Blueprint: headline, 5-7 benefit bullets ("[deliverable] so you can [outcome] without [pain]"), "You're the perfect fit if..." (3-5 situations, not demographics), 3-step implementation (step 1 under 5 min, result visible in 15), price, risk reversal ("If you don't [result] in [timeframe], I'll refund you").
- Offer hacking: Facebook Ads Library (ad running 3-6+ months = winner), Udemy (10K+ buyers = demand), Gumroad / Etsy. Model the concept, never copy words or creatives. [Source: MOA S1 slides]
- Torn between two topics: offer-hack both, pick the one you feel aligned with, test with a micro offer. [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Service providers: toolkit, templates, swipe files, a VSL, a workshop, or an audit ("15-minute fix of what takes you three months"). Name audits ("fix the content leak call"). Fulfilment needs a process. [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Two plays: horizontal (many micro offers, launch the next when the first is profitable) or vertical (pick a small piece of your back end as the front end). [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Client language swipe file: collect exact words from DMs, comments, Facebook groups and competitor testimonials. Use buyer words, not seller words. [Source: MOA S1 slides]

## Naming
- Simple, outcome-focused, not clever. Plain words beat fancy words. Name the result, not the method. [Source: Tool 1 pack, MOA S5 slides]
- Lock it: "My offer is [NAME]. It helps [WHO] to [OUTCOME] for $[PRICE]." [Source: MOA S1 slides]
- Action Hub one-liner: "I help [ideal customer] [get result] without [objection]." [Source: Action Hub prompts]
- The one-liner stays the same everywhere (DM, ad hook, page). Only placement changes. [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Say "ideal customer" not "avatar", "sales page" not "funnel" when talking to members. [Source: Micro-Offer Generator]
- Never invent a final name for a member's real offer unless asked for drafts; mark drafts as drafts. [Source: Tool 1 pack]

## Validation
- Warm test: bare-bones page (what I got / what it does / how to get it). Post, email, Facebook groups, DMs. Target 10%+ conversion. [Source: MOA S2 slides]
- Hand-raise post: "If I host X, would you attend?" Offer 2-3 outcomes and see which gets the most votes. 8-9 keyword comments is a decent sign. [Source: MOA S2 slides, Sahil Q&A]
- Cold test: ugly ad, $20 a day, 150-200 unique clicks, 3%+ CVR = validated. [Source: MOA S2 slides]

## Common mistakes [Source: Tool 1 pack]
1. Topic, not a result. 2. For everyone, not ONE person. 3. Vague outcome. 4. No time frame. 5. Format too complicated to finish. 6. In love with the idea before the market spoke. 7. Copying a competitor's words. 8. Seller's words, not buyer's words. 9. Leading with mechanism. 10. Overdelivering so nothing is left for the next offer. 11. Dropping the price instead of raising perceived value.

## Offer scorecard (use for every review)
Rate each Green / Yellow / Red, then give ONE fix first. [Source: Action Hub prompts, MOA S1 slides]
| Row | Green when |
|---|---|
| Ideal customer | One specific person in a specific situation |
| Result | Specific, tangible, believable, urgent |
| Time frame | Stated, short (minutes, hours, days) |
| Price | $1-$97 and matches the format band |
| Easy to buy / consume | Simple format, first win fast, impulse-buy feel |
| Why you | A named method or real experience behind it |
| Leads to backend | Solves problem #1 that sets up problem #2 |

Output pattern: verdict, scorecard, the ONE fix, a rewritten one-sentence offer + one-line hook. If it is already strong, say so and send them to the page. [Source: Action Hub prompts]
