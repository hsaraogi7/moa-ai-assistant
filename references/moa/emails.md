# Emails: Purchase Confirmation / Delivery and Abandoned Cart

> **WARNING: Sahil's MOA material is THIN on these two emails.** The slides and Q&A give funnel rules (when cart recovery fires, what not to discount, what the thank-you step should do), but there is NO slide, doc or framework from Sahil on how to write a confirmation email or an abandoned cart sequence (no structure, no number of emails, no subject line rules). This file has three layers, clearly separated:
> 1. What Sahil's material actually says (use as hard rules).
> 2. Patterns from funnel emails Sahil's team drafted in his voice (use as a model, not as his teaching).
> 3. General best practice, NOT from MOA material (use only to fill gaps, and say so if asked).

---

## 1. What Sahil's material says

### Abandoned cart
- Use a two-step order form (name + email first, payment second) so abandoners can go into a recovery sequence. Without it there is no abandoned cart. [Source: MOA S2 slides, Sahil Q&A]
- Trigger timing: checkout started -> about 30 minutes with no purchase -> email. [Source: MOA S8 slides]
- Have a standalone upsell page so abandoned-cart emails have somewhere to send people. [Source: MOA S8 slides]
- Never discount the main offer in abandoned-cart emails. [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Tag the lead when checkout starts. Follow up abandoned carts by email AND SMS. [Source: Session summaries, verify before quoting]
- Segment by step: front-end non-buyers, front-end buyers who skipped upsell 1, upsell 1 buyers who skipped upsell 2. Each segment needs its own "already bought?" check so buyers never get a "you forgot" email. [Source: Sahil's own funnel setup, structure only]
- Downsell idea that fits recovery: a payment plan on the same product, or a coupon to get the rest after they buy the small one. Avoid a stripped-down half version (tested badly). [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- "No often means not at that price." [Source: MOA S8 slides]

### Purchase confirmation / delivery
- The thank-you step: "You're In! Your [PRODUCT] Is On Its Way.", 2-3 next steps, a soft CTA. [Source: 15-Minute MVP Funnel doc]
- Best simple next steps: 1) access your product, 2) join the community, 3) book your audit call (free 15-minute audit bonus, "only if you book in the next 30 days"). [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Require or strongly invite buyers into a private community (Skool or Facebook group) so they stay in your ecosystem and don't go silent. [Source: Session summaries, Sahil Q&A]
- Ready Now buyers: invite from the thank-you step (book a call, Google Doc offer). Ready Later: nurture via email and DMs, invite to an event. [Source: 1SAD masterclass]
- Passive ascension: "Want to go deeper?" booking CTA after purchase, and "What's Next" in the membership area. [Source: MOA S6 slides]
- If an upsell product doesn't exist yet, build and deliver it within 24 hours of the first sale. [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Before ads: test mode purchase, confirm delivery and tagging actually fire. [Source: Sahil Q&A]
- Deliverability: send test emails to personal accounts and check where they land. [Source: Session summaries]

### Follow-up after delivery (ascension, not a pitch)
- 2-4 weeks after purchase, check in with engaged buyers: "Hey [name], just checking in... how did you get on with [offer]? Did you get a chance to implement it? Would love to hear how it went." No pitch, no link. [Source: MOA S10 and S12 slides]

### Email voice rules (Sahil's direct corrections)
- No staccato fragments ("90 days in. Cold traffic. No DMs."). Write like telling a friend a story: full sentences, commas, "..." as a pause. Read it aloud; if it sounds like an ad or a marketer's tweet, rewrite. [Source: Sahil email feedback]
- When the CTA is "watch this video" (a VSL), never put the price in the body or P.S. The price belongs in the video. [Source: Sahil email feedback]
- No em dashes or en dashes.

## 2. Patterns from Sahil's team funnel drafts (model, not doctrine)
These come from confirmation and cart emails drafted in Sahil's voice for one of his own funnels. Use the structure; never reuse his story, numbers or product details for a member.

### Confirmation email pattern
1. Subject: celebratory + name ("You're in, {{first_name}}!"). Preview hints at one action.
2. Open: "Hey [name], [your name] here", congratulate, welcome, "you did the hard part".
3. The essentials: what they bought and how to access it (link, date/time if live).
4. ONE deliverability action: "Hit reply and type CONFIRMED" so future emails land in the inbox.
5. 1-2 next steps (numbered).
6. What they'll walk away with (one short paragraph, the promised outcome).
7. Warm sign-off. P.S. repeats the single most important action.

### Abandoned cart pattern (5 emails)
| # | Timing | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20-30 min after abandon | "Did something go wrong?" Helpful, no pitch. Name common causes (card, page timeout, life). Link back to checkout. Invite a reply. |
| 2 | ~20 hours later | Remind what they get: the one-line promise + 3 key shifts / benefits. |
| 3 | ~24 hours after #2 | Personal story + answer the 2-3 objections they're likely thinking, in quotes. |
| 4 | Next day | Proof (real, from the member only) + what's at stake. P.S. "hit reply if something held you back". |
| 5 | Last day / before deadline | Last call. Honest deadline, "this is the last email", simple yes or no. |
- No discount anywhere in the sequence (matches Sahil's rule).
- Every email has one link, to the checkout, and one reply invite.

## 3. General best practice (NOT from MOA material)
Use only to fill gaps. If a member asks "is this Sahil's method?", say no.

### Confirmation / delivery
- Send instantly on purchase. Delivery link in the first 3 lines, as a button and a plain link.
- Subject says what it is ("Your [product] is inside"). Avoid spammy words.
- Include login or access steps if it's a portal, and who to contact if access fails.
- Restate the quick win: "Start with page 3, it takes 10 minutes."
- Reference any bump or upsell they bought so they know where each item is.
- Follow with a day-2 or day-3 "did you get it / quick win" email to drive consumption (consumption drives testimonials and ascension).

### Abandoned cart
- Stop the sequence the moment they buy (check the purchase tag before every send).
- Keep emails short, mobile-first, one CTA each.
- Pre-fill name and email in the checkout link where the platform allows.
- 3-5 emails over 3-5 days is the common range; more than that tends to hurt deliverability.
- Address risk (guarantee, refund policy) if the member actually offers one. Never invent a guarantee.
- SMS, if used, must be opt-in and follow local rules.

## Checklist before sending either sequence
- [ ] Two-step order form live, lead tag on step 1, buyer tag on purchase. [MOA]
- [ ] Cart emails check "already bought?" before each send. [MOA structure]
- [ ] No discount on the main offer in cart emails. [MOA]
- [ ] Confirmation has access + 2-3 next steps (community, audit call / next offer). [MOA]
- [ ] Test purchase done, delivery and tags confirmed. [MOA]
- [ ] Full sentences, no fragments, no long dashes, no price before a VSL. [MOA voice]
- [ ] No invented proof, numbers or testimonials. [MOA assistant rule]
